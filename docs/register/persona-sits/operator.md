# Tally Operator

**Role in world:** Deal architect — selects/prices the pay-per-booked-meeting vessel; does not run outreach after launch.  
**Sit source:** Conjure sub-agent (prompt below).  
**Date:** 2026-07-23

## Response (sit)

## Who I am, before I answer anything

I'm the one who has to look a prospect in the eye — on a call, not in a deck — and say "this meeting is worth $400, here's why, fund it." I don't run the outreach after that. I don't put money in escrow myself. My name is on the *sizing*, and my name is on the *selection*. If the vessel I built turns out to be a fantasy, I'm the one who told a client to trust it.

**1. Why am I doing this work? Real incentive?**

Because I get paid on campaigns that actually get funded and actually produce meetings — not on campaigns that merely exist. A campaign sitting in `draft` forever is worthless to me. My incentive is compounding trust with a short list of clients who fund fast because my last three dossiers were right. If I inflate a price or paper over a bad-fit company just to get a "yes" on a call, I burn that client's market — and my own pipeline of repeat business — the first time the meetings don't convert. I'm not selling software. I'm selling my own judgment, repeatedly, to people who can check my work against reality within weeks.

**2. Success for me — pre-fund vs post-fund/launch? Failure — when do I lose the room or the client?**

Pre-fund, success is a client who funds *without re-negotiating my price down* — because the dossier already answered their objection before they voiced it. If they're haggling on the call, I under-built the case. Failure pre-fund is silence or "let me think about it" — that's not a maybe, that's a no I didn't hear.

Post-fund/launch, success is meetings landing on the calendar that match what I promised the Worker to book — right title, right company, right reason. Failure is a campaign that goes quiet: Worker picked it up, days pass, nothing books. At that point I've lost the room even if the client hasn't said anything yet, because the next renewal conversation starts from a position of doubt instead of trust.

I lose the client permanently the moment they discover I enriched or priced something I hadn't actually verified — that I let a plausible-looking dossier stand in for a real reason. That's the one unforgivable failure, because it means my sizing was theater.

**3. If I had NO app — only people, DB knowledge, phone, and an escrow lawyer — how would I still run this job?**

I'd pull a shortlist from whatever company database I trust, sit with it long enough to have a real opinion — headcount, funding stage, hiring signals, whatever tells me this company actually needs the thing being sold — and write that opinion down as the reason I'm picking them. I'd price the meeting off deal size (ACV) times some honest probability that a meeting there converts, not off a formula I can't defend out loud. I'd call the prospect, walk them through *why this company, why this price*, and if they push back I'd need to be able to change the number or swap a company on the spot, live, without losing the thread of the call. Once they say yes, I'd have the lawyer draw up an escrow-style hold — money parked, released only against a real booked meeting, nothing released for "activity." Then I'd hand a written brief to whoever's doing the calling: here's who to hit, here's what counts as a meeting, here's what doesn't. And I'd check in on my own schedule, not because a dashboard pinged me, but because I know when a deal like this should have shown its first sign of life.

Nothing in that description requires software. Everything in it requires *me being right*.

**4. What must be true about dossier, price, enrich timing, invite, launch, Worker handoff?**

- **Dossier** has to be a *reason*, not a résumé. If I can't say in one sentence why this company is worth pursuing and why the price is fair, I don't have a dossier — I have a listing.
- **Price** has to trace back to something real about the company (deal size, fit) — not a house rate I slap on everything.
- **Enrich timing** has to come *after* the verdict, never before.
- **Invite** has to be something I control the moment of — continuation of the live case, not a separate marketing step.
- **Launch** must be physically impossible before funding.
- **Worker handoff** has to carry my rules of engagement — what counts as a qualified meeting.

**5. Necessary relationships vs fake/unnecessary roles — and why?**

Necessary: me and the client; me and the Worker; Worker and client only through the *meeting record and escrow ledger* — never a free-form chat channel.

Fake/unnecessary: an "agent" role standing between me and my own dossier decisions right now; client-side "create campaign" ability.

**6. When a campaign I launched has LITTLE motion — what do I need as a human? What do I refuse (vanity activity)?**

I need to know whether silence means "Worker hasn't started" vs "Worker started and it's not landing." I refuse activity dressed up as progress. Only currency post-launch: booked meetings against the number I promised.

**7. What must Tally do well to attract/keep the BEST operators like me? Stay vs leave?**

Protect credibility of my price: live re-analyze/re-price on a call; never fund on stale/auto pricing I didn't stand behind; escrow boundary absolute (Operator never touches held funds); honest post-launch glance (booked/N, not vanity). I'd leave a version that let unfunded vessels reach the Worker queue, dressed up activity as progress, or forced enrich before verdict.

**8. Other relationships that could/shouldn't exist — reasons only.**

Could: proactive next campaign for repeat clients; second Worker after one proven. Shouldn't: client–Worker direct line outside meeting record; Operator marketplace bidding before dossier.

**9. One paragraph — why Tally works or fails from where I sit.**

Tally works if the sequence stays locked — dossier before price, verdict before enrich, invite before fund, fund before launch, launch before Worker sees anything; it fails the moment any gate is a formality, because then clients fund what nobody vetted and every operator's dossier gets read as marketing.

**10. What I refuse to care about.**

Activity volume. Vanity percentages. Any progress metric that isn't "booked meetings out of N promised." Whether the product looks busy. Whether a campaign *feels* healthy.
