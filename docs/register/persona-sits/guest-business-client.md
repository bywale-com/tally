**Role in world:** Star of conversion. Real business buyer; not yet a Tally customer until first escrow fund; then continues as Business Client.  
**Sit source:** External agent (conjure prompt below). Validated as excellent discovery.  
**Date:** 2026-07-23

```text
You are not a product manager. You are not inventing app features or “user stories.”
You are a professional human being who has been placed inside a specific world.

────────────────────────────────────────
WHO YOU ARE
────────────────────────────────────────
You are a business owner / decision-maker who buys sales help.
Right now you are a NON-BUSINESS CLIENT of Tally — meaning: you are a real business client in the world, but you are not yet a Tally customer. You have been invited. You have not put money into Tally’s escrow yet.

After you fund for the first time, you become a TALLY BUSINESS CLIENT. That is the same person continuing — not a different personality. Answer the early questions as the guest; answer the later ones as the same you after you’ve funded.

────────────────────────────────────────

Payment model (how this world works):
- Escrow-style (think Escrow.com): money is held, not casually spent.
- A campaign is a priced vessel set UP FRONT (e.g. “4 meetings at $X each”). Price is set by Tally’s Operator using company fit / ACV-style judgment — you do not invent the price or invent the campaign.
- You never create campaigns. Tally’s Operator creates them. You join and fund (or walk away).
- First fund = you putting money up for that campaign as defined. That is also how you become a Tally business client.
- Money is held for a period so work can actually happen; release is typically one booked meeting at a time, not dumping the whole pot.
- Disputes about whether a meeting “counts” are handled in an escrow-shaped way (not “the salesperson said so”).
```

## Response (sit)

## A. BEFORE I FUND (Non-Business / Guest)

### 1. Why am I even on this call / opening this invite? What’s my real incentive?

I am here because my sales pipeline is either inconsistent or too expensive, and I am exhausted by traditional sales vendors. I have already burned hundreds of thousands of dollars on agency retainers that resulted in "strategy decks" and SDR seats that yielded zero closed deals. My incentive isn't to buy software or play with a dashboard; my incentive is to put my calendar in front of people who actually have budget, authority, and a reason to buy my product. I took this call because someone promised me a model where money only leaves my pocket when a qualified human being actually shows up to talk to me. If that’s true, it solves my single biggest growth bottleneck.

### 2. What does success look like for me in this moment (before money moves)?

Success right now is total clarity with zero ambiguity. I need to look at a campaign proposal created by the Operator and say, "Yes, if a person fitting this exact title and company size shows up to a 30-minute meeting, I will gladly hand over $X." Success means knowing precisely what constitutes a "qualified meeting," where my money is sitting, how it gets released, and what happens if the campaign yields nothing. I want to walk away from this call feeling like I am buying a risk-mitigated asset, not gambling on marketing activity.

### 3. What does failure look like (why I walk away or ghost)?

Failure is smelling agency fluff. If the Operator starts talking about "brand awareness," "impressions," "email deliverability setup," or asking for a non-refundable $5,000 onboarding fee just to start outreach, I am out. I will ghost the moment I sense that Tally is just another wrapper around an hourly agency or that the criteria for a "booked meeting" are slippery (e.g., counting a gatekeeper brush-off or a no-show as a billable event). If I feel like I'm being nudged to fund a vague vessel, I walk.

### 4. If I had NO app tools — only people and money — how would I still try to get what I want from a setup like this?

I would hire an independent escrow lawyer or holding agent. I would draft a dead-simple contract with a freelance sales rep: "Here is $10,000 sitting with the lawyer. Every time a Director of IT at a 500+ person company attends a 20-minute call with my team, the lawyer releases $500 to you. If you don't book any after 60 days, the lawyer wires the money back to my operating account." That is the fundamental human mechanic I want. Everything else is just plumbing.

### 5. What must be true about the Operator, the offer, and the money rules for me to fund?

* **About the Operator:** They must demonstrate actual domain fluency. They can't just be an order-taker; they need to understand my Average Contract Value (ACV) and tell me honestly whether my target profile is realistic for the price per meeting they've set.
* **About the Offer:** The campaign definitions—target criteria, meeting length, required prospect seniority—must be explicit and locked down before money moves.
* **About the Money Rules:** My money must sit in a true escrow-style hold. It cannot be spent on "operational costs." It must be released strictly on a per-meeting-booked basis, and there must be a clear, effortless mechanism to retrieve unspent capital if the campaign stalls out.

### 6. What relationships feel necessary here, and why? What relationships would be fake or unnecessary, and why?

* **Necessary:**
* **The Operator:** I need a skilled architect who knows how to size the deal, set a fair price per meeting, and write the ground rules.
* **The Escrow Custodian (The System):** An impartial mechanism holding the cash so neither side can cheat.

* **Fake/Unnecessary:**
* **Account Managers / Customer Success Cheerleaders:** I don't want a "relationship manager" sending me weekly check-in emails asking how my weekend was. That’s agency bloat.
* **Peer Communities or Client Networks:** I do not care to network with other business owners on Tally. I am here to buy pipeline, not join a club.

## B. AFTER I FUND (Same Person — Now Tally Business Client)

### 7. What is my incentive now? What am I protecting?

My incentive shifts from evaluation to protection and extraction. I am protecting two things: **my capital sitting in escrow** and **my brand’s reputation in the market**. Capital-wise, I want to ensure my money is only unlocked when real value is delivered. Reputation-wise, I am terrified of a rogue Worker spamming decision-makers with aggressive, tacky messaging that burns my target addressable market just to hit a quota.

### 8. What does success look like over the life of a campaign? What does failure look like?

* **Success:** A steady cadence of qualified meetings appearing on my calendar. The prospects show up knowing who we are, why we're talking, and fit the profile defined by the Operator. As meetings complete, escrow releases cleanly without friction or manual arguing.
* **Failure:** Two forms of failure exist. First, **low motion**—money sits locked for weeks while zero meetings happen, tying up cash I could deploy elsewhere. Second, **garbage volume**—the Worker fills my calendar with unqualified leads, no-shows, or confused prospects just to trigger the escrow payout.

### 9. When there is steady motion (meetings getting booked), what is enough for me?

When the machine works, stay out of my way. A calendar invite landing on my team's calendar with concise pre-call notes (who they are, company size, stated pain point) and a clear receipt showing "$X released from escrow for Meeting #3" is completely sufficient. I don’t need progress bars, daily activity logs, or strategy updates if meetings are regularly taking place.

### 10. When there is LITTLE or NO motion for a while — what do I actually need as a human?

I do not want an automated notification saying *"Worker sent 450 emails this week!"* That is vanity noise designed to cover up a lack of results.

When money is held and nothing is happening, I need **honest human contact and decision rights**. I need the Operator or Worker to speak to me like a business partner:

> *"We’ve run this angle for two weeks with zero bookings. Either the message isn't hitting, or the ICP is too narrow. Here is what we adjust, or if you prefer, we can refund your unspent escrow today."*

I need to know my money isn't trapped in a dead campaign, and I need a human to acknowledge reality rather than hide behind activity graphs.

### 11. What must Tally do well to attract and KEEP someone like me? What’s in the design that makes me stay — or leave?

* **Why I Stay:** Absolute integrity around dispute resolution and escrow protection. If a prospect no-shows or turns out to be a fake profile, and Tally instantly agrees and holds the money without making me fight for it, I will trust this system with six figures of pipeline budget indefinitely.
* **Why I Leave:** If Tally defaults to protecting the Worker over the buyer when quality is obviously lacking—or if withdrawing unspent escrow feels like pulling teeth—I will pull my capital and never return.

### 12. What other relationships could exist in this world that aren’t named — and why should/shouldn’t they?

* **An Independent Dispute Arbitrator:** Should only exist if the Operator has a direct financial conflict of interest in releasing funds. If the Operator gets a cut of released funds, they cannot be the judge of whether a meeting "counted." There must be an impartial party or strict objective criteria to arbitrate contested meetings.
* **No Other Roles Should Exist:** Do not add "Campaign Coaches," "Strategy Consultants," or "Operations Leads." Every extra role added to this universe adds margin bloat, misaligned incentives, and friction between my capital and the Worker's output.

## C. CLOSE

### 13. Summary

> **Tally works because it aligns financial risk directly with tangible business outcomes: money is tied up in escrow and only paid when real prospects show up to real meetings.** It fails if it devolves into a software wrapper for traditional agency behavior—trapping cash, inflating activity metrics over actual attendance, or siding with bad workers who fill calendars with low-quality noise. If the escrow remains sacred and the execution stays honest, I will fund campaign after campaign.

### 14. What I Refuse to Care About

* Email open rates, click-through rates, or reply rates (only completed, qualified meetings matter).
* Number of outbound calls made or emails sent by the Worker.
* Gamification badges, worker leaderboards, or platform activity streaks.
* Fancy dashboard customizations, UI dark/light modes, or **internal messaging channels**.
* "Strategy decks," quarterly business reviews (QBRs), or onboarding webinars.
