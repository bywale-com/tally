## What this pond is

NAICS **56173 Landscaping services**: establishments providing landscape care/maintenance and/or installing trees, shrubs, lawns, gardens, and (when not classified to construction) walkways, retaining walls, decks, fences, ponds. ISED 2024 SME snapshot: 19,115 Canadian businesses in the $30k–$5M band, average revenue **$488.9k**, average net profit **$55.7k** (profitable firms **$87.2k**), **77%** profitable. IBISWorld 2026 Canada: **$20.5bn**, **26,581** businesses.

Ontario listing counts disagree by source (POIData **3,080**; companydata **4,888**). Landscape Ontario is a reachable subset: **2,622** members, of which ~1,717 are Green Trade Member / sole prop / intro (the rest are suppliers/individuals). Toronto is LO’s largest chapter.

Public 2026 GTA interlock/landscape tickets are mid-four to mid-five figures. Association charge-out for skilled landscape roles is already in the $75–150/h band (2022 survey; Landscape Trades 2024 survey said ~¾ of respondents raised prices in 2024 — https://landscapeontario.com/assets/1732736511.December_2024_LT_State_of_the_Industry.pdf).

The pond can be entered as (a) a tiny operator *among* these owners, or (b) a caller *into* these owners as buyers of ancillary technical work. Both populations are the same phone list.

## What we are not claiming

No invented proposition. No Deep Screener scenario. No “you should sell X” to landscapers. Charge-out and ticket prices prove **pond economics**, not problem-market fit.

## Problem-class hypotheses (untested, not admission criteria)

- Sub-trade coordination around locates, drainage, and hardscape base spec (untested)
- Seasonal cash-flow / snow attachment (untested as a *lived* owner problem)
- Quote/spec variance on interlock base depth and demolition line items (untested; appears in contractor marketing)
- Labour/recruitment pressure (untested as a buyer of services; stated in Landscape Trades surveys)
