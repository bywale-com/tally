Certainty in this plan does **not** mean certainty that candidate #1 works. This is tolerable:

> test → kill → test → kill → test → winner.

What needs confidence is the **search space upstream**.

If we establish that a market is:

- sufficiently large for one tiny operator;
- economically capable of producing $3k net within 30 hours;
- populated by reachable buyers;
- capable of supporting the required price/unit economics;
- technically serviceable by January;
- and contains enough heterogeneous problems that we're not betting everything on one imagined scenario,

then ambiguity at the **proposition layer** is tolerable.

October calling into it and discovering “nope / they don't care / they all complain about *that* / they already have somebody / this one keeps repeating” is not wandering. It is **searching a bounded, economically qualified problem space for the outbound winner.**

Not affordable:

> “I'll spend three months exploring and maybe there's a business somewhere.”

Tolerable:

> **“We've established that this pond contains enough economic value. I don't yet know which fish I'm catching.”**

Tally analogue: the scanner does not need to know which company converts. It needs a sufficiently good hunting ground and a ruthless elimination mechanism.

Once market certainty is high enough, tolerate a lot of the second variable while hunting.
